Triangles and trig

The Pythagorean theorem, special right triangles, SOH-CAH-TOA and the geometry formulas precalc assumes you know.

5.1Pythagorean theorem and distance

In a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c (the side across from the right angle): a² + b² = c².

a² + b² = c²

Pythagorean triples

3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25 and their multiples (6-8-10, 9-12-15…).

Distance formula

It's just Pythagoras on the grid: d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²). Midpoint = average the coordinates.

5.2Special right triangles

45°-45°-90°

Sides 1 : 1 : √2. A square cut on its diagonal. Legs x, hypotenuse x√2.

30°-60°-90°

Sides 1 : √3 : 2. Half an equilateral triangle. Short leg x (across from 30°), long leg x√3, hypotenuse 2x.
Worked example: A 30-60-90 triangle has hypotenuse 14. Find both legs.
Short leg = 14/2 = 7. Long leg = 7√3 ≈ 12.1.
Why these matterThese two triangles give you the exact values on the unit circle in precalc: sin 30° = 1/2, cos 45° = √2/2, sin 60° = √3/2.

5.3SOH-CAH-TOA

For an acute angle θ in a right triangle:

RatioDefinitionMemory
sin θopposite / hypotenuseSOH
cos θadjacent / hypotenuseCAH
tan θopposite / adjacentTOA

In the unit circle above, the hypotenuse is 1, so the legs are cos θ (horizontal) and sin θ (vertical). That's the bridge into precalc.

To find an angle, use the inverse: if tan θ = 3/4, then θ = tan⁻¹(3/4) ≈ 36.9°.

Calculator modeDegrees vs radians is the #1 lost-points mistake. sin 30 in radian mode gives −0.988, not 0.5.

5.4Area, volume and circles

ShapeFormula
TriangleA = ½bh
CircleA = πr², C = 2πr
TrapezoidA = ½(b₁ + b₂)h
CylinderV = πr²h
ConeV = ⅓πr²h
SphereV = ⁴⁄₃πr³, S = 4πr²
Rectangular boxV = lwh

A circle with center (h, k) and radius r: (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². It's the distance formula again.

Arc length and sector area are fractions of the whole circle: for a central angle θ°, arc = (θ/360)·2πr.
