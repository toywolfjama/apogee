3.1Periodic functions, radians and the unit circle (3.1–3.3)

A function is periodic with period k if f(x + k) = f(x): the pattern repeats every k units. Tides, daylight, Ferris wheels.

A radian is the angle that cuts off an arc equal to the radius. A full turn is 2π radians = 360°, so multiply degrees by π/180.

Radians0π/6π/4π/3π/2π3π/22π

On the unit circle, the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ). So sin θ is the height, cos θ is the horizontal position, and tan θ = sin θ / cos θ is the slope of the terminal ray. Press the preset angles below and watch the exact values.

3.8Tangent, inverse trig and the reciprocal functions (3.8–3.11)

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ. It's undefined where cos θ = 0, so tan has vertical asymptotes at θ = π/2 + kπ, and its period is π.

InverseRestricted domain of the originalOutput range
sin⁻¹ x (arcsin)[−π/2, π/2][−π/2, π/2]
cos⁻¹ x (arccos)[0, π][0, π]
tan⁻¹ x (arctan)(−π/2, π/2)(−π/2, π/2)

The restrictions make each one-to-one. That's why sin⁻¹(1/2) = π/6 only, while sin θ = 1/2 has infinitely many solutions.

Reciprocals

sec θ = 1/cos θ, csc θ = 1/sin θ, cot θ = cos θ/sin θ. They have asymptotes wherever the original is 0.

Inequalities

Solve the equation, mark the solutions on the circle or graph, then test which arcs satisfy the inequality.

3.13Polar coordinates and graphs (3.13–3.15)

A polar point (r, θ) means: turn θ, then go r. Convert with x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, r² = x² + y², tan θ = y/x.

A polar function r = f(θ) is graphed by sweeping θ. In the labs below, the left panel is the polar curve and the right panel is the same function drawn as r vs θ. Distance from the origin increases where r is positive and increasing (or negative and decreasing).

Unit summary

Unit circle

(cos θ, sin θ). 360° = 2π. ASTC for signs.

Sinusoids

a sin(b(x + c)) + d: amplitude |a|, period 2π/|b|, midline y = d.

Tangent & inverses

tan period π, asymptotes at π/2 + kπ. Inverse trig outputs are restricted.

Polar

x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ. Distance grows when r and its rate share a sign.
