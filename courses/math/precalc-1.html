AP Precalculus · Unit 1

Polynomial and rational functions

Rates of change, polynomials and their zeros, rational functions and asymptotes, and choosing a model.

1.1Rates of change (1.1–1.3)

Precalc is about how outputs change as inputs change. The average rate of change of f on [a, b] is the slope of the secant line:

AROC = (f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a)

As the interval shrinks, the secant line approaches the tangent line and the average rate approaches the rate of change at a point (that's the derivative you'll meet in calculus).

BehaviorMeans
Increasingas x increases, f(x) increases (rate > 0)
Concave upthe rate of change is increasing (graph bends like a cup)
Concave downthe rate of change is decreasing (graph bends like a frown)
Point of inflectionwhere concavity switches

Linear functions have a constant rate of change. Quadratic functions have rates that change at a constant rate: over equal input steps, the first differences form a linear pattern, and the second differences are constant.

1.4Polynomial functions (1.4–1.6)

A polynomial of degree n: p(x) = aₙxⁿ + … + a₁x + a₀. The leading term controls the end behavior.

DegreeLeading coefficientEnd behavior
even+↑ on both ends
even−↓ on both ends
odd+↙ down on the left, ↗ up on the right
odd−↖ up on the left, ↘ down on the right

Zeros and multiplicity. A factor (x − r)k gives a zero at r with multiplicity k. Odd multiplicity: the graph crosses. Even multiplicity: it bounces (touches and turns).

Turning points

A degree-n polynomial has at most n − 1 turning points (local max/min) and at most n real zeros.

Complex zeros

Degree n means exactly n complex zeros counting multiplicity. Non-real zeros come in conjugate pairs: if 2 + 3i is a zero, so is 2 − 3i.

Even function

f(−x) = f(x). Symmetric over the y-axis. Example x⁴ − 3x².

Odd function

f(−x) = −f(x). Symmetric about the origin. Example x³ − 5x.

1.7Rational functions (1.7–1.10)

A rational function is a ratio r(x) = p(x)/q(x). Factor first, then read off its features:

FeatureWhere it comes from
Zerosx-values that make the numerator 0 (and not the denominator)
Vertical asymptotea factor of the denominator that does NOT cancel
Holea factor that cancels from top and bottom
Horizontal asymptotecompare degrees: top < bottom → y = 0; equal → ratio of leading coefficients; top > bottom → none (slant if one more)

End behavior with limits: as x → ∞, r(x) → (horizontal asymptote). Near a vertical asymptote, r(x) → ±∞; test a value on each side to get the sign.

Hole or asymptote?Cancel common factors first! (x − 2)/(x − 2)(x + 2) has a hole at 2, not an asymptote.

1.11Equivalent forms and transformations (1.11–1.12)

Polynomial long division rewrites a rational function as quotient + remainder/divisor, which reveals a slant asymptote.

Worked example: Divide (x² + 3x + 5) ÷ (x + 1).
x·(x + 1) = x² + x, leaving 2x + 5. Then 2(x + 1) = 2x + 2, leaving 3. So x + 2 + 3/(x + 1): slant asymptote y = x + 2.

Binomial theorem: expand (a + b)ⁿ with Pascal's triangle coefficients. (x + 2)³ = x³ + 3·2x² + 3·4x + 8 = x³ + 6x² + 12x + 8.

Transformations: g(x) = a·f(b(x + c)) + d. The domain and range shift and scale with them.

1.13Choosing and building models (1.13–1.14)

On the AP exam you pick a model from the data or the context, then justify it.

Clue in the dataModel
constant first differenceslinear
constant second differencesquadratic
constant third differencescubic
a few known zeros, a turning shapepolynomial built from factors
output → constant as input grows, blows up near a valuerational
constant ratios over equal stepsexponential (Unit 2)
Worked example: A cubic has zeros at x = −1, 2, and 5 and passes through (0, 20). Find it.
p(x) = a(x + 1)(x − 2)(x − 5). At 0: a(1)(−2)(−5) = 10a = 20 → a = 2.
JustifyFree-response answers need a reason in context: "The second differences are all 4, so a quadratic model is appropriate."

Unit summary

Rates

AROC = slope of secant. Concave up ⇔ rate increasing.

Polynomials

Leading term → end behavior. Multiplicity: odd crosses, even bounces. ≤ n zeros, ≤ n − 1 turns.

Rational

Factor! Cancelled → hole. Leftover bottom → VA. Degrees → HA or slant.

Models

Differences pick the polynomial degree. Build from zeros + one point.
