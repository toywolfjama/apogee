1Reading and Writing: a tactic for every question type

When the question says…Do thisWatch out for
"most logical and precise word"Cover the choices, predict a word from the clue in the sentence, then match.Words with the right vibe but wrong meaning; second meanings.
"function of the underlined sentence" / "main purpose"Ask: what job does it do? Use a verb (contrasts, illustrates, introduces).Choices that describe content instead of function.
"overall structure"Label each sentence in 2–3 words, then match the sequence.Right moves in the wrong order.
Text 1 / Text 2Summarize each author's claim in 5 words. Agree, disagree, or partly?Choices that only restate one text.
"main idea"What would the author say in one sentence?True details that are too narrow; claims too broad.
"most directly support/weaken"Restate the exact claim. The answer must hit its key word.Related facts that don't touch the claim.
Graph/table + claimFind the claim's two parts; the answer must be true in the data AND match the claim.Accurate data that answers a different question.
"most logically completes the text"Follow the chain of reasoning; pick what MUST follow.Choices that might be true but go further.
Grammar ("conventions")Look at what changes between choices. Identify the rule: boundary? agreement? tense?Picking what "sounds right" instead of applying the rule.
TransitionName the relationship before looking (contrast, cause, example, addition).Two choices that mean the same thing: both are wrong.
Notes / "accomplish this goal"Underline the goal. Ignore everything else.Well-written sentences that don't meet the goal.
Two-choice tiebreakDown to two? Find the single word that makes one of them wrong. Right answers are boring and fully supported; wrong answers have one word too strong ("always," "proves," "only").

2Math: seven tactics that save time

1Read the last line first

Circle what they actually ask for: x, 2x + 1, the y-intercept, the number of solutions. Many wrong choices are the right answer to a different question.

2Plug in numbers

Variables in the answer choices? Pick easy numbers (x = 2, not 0 or 1), compute the answer, then test each choice.

3Backsolve

Numbers in the choices and a question like "what is x?" Try the middle choice in the problem; adjust up or down.

4Desmos it

Graphs, systems, quadratics, tables. See the Desmos tactics page.

5Look for the shortcut

If they ask for 6x + 4 and you know 3x + 2 = 7, the answer is 14. Don't solve for x.

6Units and labels

Write units on every number in a word problem. Answers in the wrong units are planted traps (minutes vs hours, cm vs m).

7Sanity check

Does the answer make sense? A probability above 1, a negative length, or a sale price above the original means a mistake.

4Module strategy and test day

Module 1 decides a lot

Your Module 1 performance sets the difficulty of Module 2, and only the harder Module 2 can reach top scores. Slow down a little and double-check Module 1.

Use the tools

Flag questions, cross out choices (the strikethrough tool), and use the on-screen timer. Hide the timer if it stresses you; it warns you at 5 minutes.

Two passes

Pass 1: answer everything you can in under ~90 seconds. Pass 2: flagged questions. Last minute: no blanks.

Energy

Use the 10-minute break: water, snack, walk. Math comes second, when you're most tired.
