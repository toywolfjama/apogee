6.1Ratios, rates, percents and units

Percent change

(new − old)/old × 100. From 40 to 50 is +25%; from 50 to 40 is −20%.

Percent of a percent

Two changes multiply: +20% then −20% gives 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% drop.

Unit conversions

Multiply by fractions equal to 1 so units cancel: 90 km/h × (1000 m/1 km) × (1 h/3600 s) = 25 m/s.

Proportions

Set up equal ratios and cross-multiply. Keep units in the same places.

6.2Statistics and data

Mean = sum/count. Median = middle value. Range = max − min. Standard deviation measures spread: data bunched near the mean → small SD.

OutliersAdding a very large value raises the mean a lot but the median only a little. That's why income is usually described by the median.

Try to get the line through the middle of the dots (about m = 7, b = 20). Hours studied vs score: each extra hour ≈ +7 points.

Sampling

Results from a random sample can be generalized to the population it was drawn from, and no further.

Cause vs association

Only a randomized experiment (random assignment to treatments) supports cause-and-effect conclusions.

Margin of error

An estimate of 42% ± 3% means the true value is plausibly between 39% and 45%. Bigger samples → smaller margin.

6.3Probability from tables

P(event) = favorable / total. In a two-way table, the hard part is picking the right total: "given that" means restrict to that row or column.

Worked example: If a Grade 12 student is chosen at random, what's the probability they like hiking?
Restrict to the Grade 12 row: 45/70 = 9/14 ≈ 0.643.
(Of all 150 students, P(Grade 12 and likes hiking) = 45/150 = 0.3: a different question.)

6.4Geometry: angles, triangles, area and volume

A reference sheet in the app gives area and volume formulas, the special right triangles, and that a circle has 360° (2π radians). You still need to know when to use them.

Angles

Straight line 180°. Triangle angles sum to 180°. Parallel lines cut by a transversal: alternate interior angles are equal.

Similar triangles

Same angles → sides in the same ratio. If sides scale by k, area scales by k² and volume by k³.

Right triangles

a² + b² = c². 30-60-90: x, x√3, 2x. 45-45-90: x, x, x√2.

Volume

Prism/cylinder: base area × height. Cone/pyramid: ⅓ of that. Sphere: ⁴⁄₃πr³.
Worked example: Two similar triangles have corresponding sides 4 and 10. The smaller has area 12. Area of the larger?
Scale factor 10/4 = 2.5; area scales by 2.5² = 6.25. 12 × 6.25 = 75.

6.5Right-triangle trig and circles

SOH-CAH-TOA

sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj. Favorite SAT fact: sin x° = cos(90 − x)°.

Radians

180° = π radians. Arc length = rθ (θ in radians). Sector area = ½r²θ.

Circle equation

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r². If it's expanded, complete the square in x and in y to find the center and radius.
