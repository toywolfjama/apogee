1.1How the SAT works

The SAT is taken on a laptop or tablet in the Bluebook app. It has two sections, each split into two timed modules.

Adaptive modules

Module 1 is a mix of easy, medium and hard questions. How you do there decides whether Module 2 is the harder or the easier version. Only the harder Module 2 can lead to top scores, so Module 1 accuracy matters a lot.

Scoring

Each section is scored 200–800; the total is 400–1600. A few questions in each module are unscored pretest items, but you can't tell which, so treat every question as real.

No guessing penalty

A wrong answer costs the same as a blank. Never leave anything blank.

Calculator

A graphing calculator (Desmos) is built into the app for the whole Math section, and you can bring an approved one. A reference sheet of geometry formulas is built in too.

Inside a module you can skip around, flag questions, and cross out choices. Once a module's time is up you can't go back to it.

About 75% of Math questions are multiple choice. The rest are student-produced responses (grid-ins): you type the answer.

Grid-in rulesPositive answers can be up to 5 characters (6 for negative, counting the minus sign). Use a fraction or a decimal, not a mixed number: 3½ must be entered as 7/2 or 3.5. If a decimal runs long, fill all the spaces: 2/3 can be 2/3, .6666, .6667, 0.666 or 0.667. If there's more than one right answer, enter just one.

1.3Strategy that actually raises scores

  1. Read the question first on Reading and Writing, then the passage. You'll know what to look for.
  2. Predict before you peek. Say the answer in your own words, then find the choice that matches. Trap answers are built to sound good.
  3. Eliminate. Cross out every choice with one wrong word. On hard questions you often find the right answer by being sure the other three are wrong.
  4. Two-pass each module. If a question takes more than about 2 minutes, flag it, guess, and move on. Come back at the end.
  5. Use Desmos aggressively. Graph both sides of an equation and read the intersection; type a system in and click the crossing point.
  6. Plug in numbers when answer choices have variables: pick x = 2, compute, and test each choice.
  7. Backsolve when choices are numbers: start with a middle value and check it in the problem.
