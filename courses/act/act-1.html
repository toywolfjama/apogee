The ACT, decoded

How the enhanced ACT is built and scored, what each section tests, and how to pace yourself.

1.1How the ACT works

The current (enhanced) ACT can be taken online or on paper. You pick whether to add Science and Writing when you register.

Scores

Each section is scored 1–36. Your Composite is the rounded average of English, Math and Reading.

Math choices

Math questions have four answer choices (the old test had five). A calculator is allowed on Math only: the digital ACT has Desmos built in; on paper, bring your own.

No guessing penalty

Blank = wrong. Fill in every answer, even with seconds left.

Not adaptive

Unlike the SAT, every student gets the same questions. Earlier questions in Math tend to be easier.
SAT or ACT?Both are accepted everywhere that accepts test scores. The ACT gives less time per question but is not adaptive; the SAT gives more time and a built-in graphing calculator. Take one practice test of each and go with the one you score better on.

1.2What each section tests

SectionWhat it covers
EnglishPassages with underlined parts. Fix punctuation, grammar and sentence structure (about 40%), plus writing strategy, organization and style (about 60%: "Production of Writing" and "Knowledge of Language").
MathPre-algebra through precalc topics: number and quantity, algebra, functions, geometry, statistics and probability, plus some matrices, logs, complex numbers and vectors.
ReadingSeveral passages (literary narrative, social science, humanities, natural science), including one pair of shorter passages. Main idea, details, inference, word meaning, author's purpose.
ScienceGraphs, tables and experiments in biology, chemistry, physics, earth science and engineering. It's mostly reading data, not memorized facts. Some passages present conflicting viewpoints.
WritingOne prompt about an issue with three perspectives. You take a position and support it.

1.3Strategy

  1. English: shorter is usually better. When choices are all grammatically correct, pick the most concise one that keeps the meaning. "DELETE" is right more often than you'd think.
  2. Math: skip and return. Every question is worth the same. Don't spend 4 minutes on question 12.
  3. Reading: find proof. The right answer is backed by a specific line. If you can't point to it, it's probably a trap.
  4. Science: data first. Read axis labels and units. Many questions need only one figure.
  5. Fill every bubble. With 1 minute left, guess on everything blank. Pick one letter and use it for all your guesses.
