ACT · Part 7

The essay, planned

How the optional ACT Writing essay is scored, a 40-minute plan, a model outline and a timed practice prompt.

7.1The task

Optional, 40 minutes, one essay. You get a short description of an issue and three perspectives on it. You write an argument that states your own position and analyzes how it relates to at least one other perspective.

Domain (each scored 2–12)What earns points
Ideas and AnalysisA clear, precise thesis; engaging with the given perspectives, including the tension between them
Development and SupportReasons and specific examples that explain why your position holds, plus limits or counterarguments
OrganizationA logical structure with transitions; each paragraph has one job
Language Use and ConventionsClear, varied sentences, precise words, few errors

Two readers score each domain 1–6; the two scores add to 2–12. Your Writing score is the rounded average of the four domain scores.

7.2A 40-minute plan

MinutesDo
0–8Read the prompt and perspectives. Choose a position (it can combine perspectives). List 2–3 reasons with one concrete example each. Note one counterargument.
8–14Introduction: context in one sentence, then a thesis that names your position and your main reasons.
14–34Body paragraphs: claim → explanation → example → link back to thesis. One paragraph addresses another perspective and shows its limits.
34–38Conclusion: restate the position more broadly; why it matters.
38–40Proofread for missing words and unclear sentences.

7.3Practice prompt and model outline

Issue: Automation at work. More jobs are being done by software and machines. Some people welcome the change; others worry about workers left behind.

Perspective One: Automation frees people from dull, repetitive work so they can focus on creative and human tasks.

Perspective Two: Automation helps companies but harms workers, whose skills become worthless overnight.

Perspective Three: Whether automation helps or harms depends on whether society invests in retraining workers.

Worked example: Model thesis
Automation can free people from repetitive work, as Perspective One argues, but only when communities pay for retraining; without it, Perspective Two's warning comes true. That's why the investment in Perspective Three decides the outcome.

Body 1

Benefit: a factory that automated packing moved workers into maintenance roles, with higher pay, because it trained them.

Body 2

Risk: when a town's mill automated without retraining, workers lost jobs; this shows Perspective Two's concern is real, not inevitable.

Body 3 / counter

Someone might say retraining is too slow or costly. But the cost of unemployment (lost taxes, public aid) is often higher.

Conclusion

Automation itself is neither good nor bad. The choice to invest in people is what decides.

Timed essay

One essay, 40 minutes, then self-score with the rubric.
