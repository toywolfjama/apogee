ACT English, rule by rule

Punctuation, grammar and usage, sentence structure, and the strategy and style questions that make up most of the section.

2.0How ACT English works

You read passages with parts underlined. For each, pick the best version; choice A is usually NO CHANGE. Some questions ask about the whole passage: adding or deleting a sentence, ordering paragraphs, or whether the essay met a goal.

Three checks, in order

1) Is it grammatically correct? 2) Is it clear and concise? 3) Does it fit the paragraph's meaning and tone?

Scan the choices first

What changes between them tells you the rule being tested: commas? verb tense? wordiness?

2.1Punctuation

MarkRuleExample
Commaafter intro elements; around nonessential info; before FANBOYS joining two sentences; between list itemsAfter lunch, we left. / My sister, a pilot, flew home.
Semicolonbetween two complete sentences (works like a period)I was late; the bus broke down.
Colonafter a complete sentence, introducing a list, explanation or quoteShe had one goal: to win.
Dashlike commas or a colon, but in matching pairs for interruptionsThe answer — surprisingly — was no.
Apostrophepossession or contraction (no apostrophe for plain plurals)the dog's bowl, the dogs' bowls, it's = it is

2.2Grammar and usage

Agreement

Subject ↔ verb, pronoun ↔ noun. Skip the prepositional phrase to find the subject.

Verb tense

Match the passage's time frame. Don't switch from past to present mid-paragraph without a reason.

Pronoun case

Subject: I, he, she, we, they, who. Object: me, him, her, us, them, whom. "Between you and me."

Comparisons

Two things: -er / more. Three or more: -est / most. Compare like with like: "Her score was higher than Jo's" (not "than Jo").

Idioms

Preposition pairs: capable of, interested in, different from, comply with.

Adjective vs adverb

Adverbs describe verbs: she sang beautifully, not beautiful.

2.3Sentence structure

Run-ons and splices

Two sentences need a period, semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS.

Fragments

Every sentence needs a subject and a main verb. "Because it rained." is a fragment.

Modifiers

Put describing phrases next to what they describe.

Parallelism

Lists match in form: "to run, to swim, and to bike," not "and biking."

2.4Strategy, organization and style

These are most of the English section. They ask what the writer should do.

Question typeHow to answer
"Should the writer add this sentence?"Yes only if it directly supports the paragraph's point. Pick the choice with the right reason.
"If the writer deleted this, the paragraph would lose…"Name exactly what the sentence does (an example, a transition, a definition).
"Which choice best introduces/concludes the paragraph?"Read the whole paragraph; the right choice previews or sums up its main idea.
"Where should sentence 4 be placed?"Look for pronouns and time words that link sentences: "this," "then," "after that."
"Which choice is most concise?" / redundancyCut repeated meanings: "in my opinion I think," "annual yearly."
TransitionsName the relationship first: contrast, cause, example, addition, time.
"Suppose the writer's goal was…"Did the essay do exactly that? Choose yes/no + the reason that matches the essay.
