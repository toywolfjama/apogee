3Reading tactics

Order your passages

Do your favorite passage type first (narrative, science, etc.). You choose the order on test day.

Skim, then answer

2–3 minutes skimming: main idea of each paragraph and the author's tone. Then go to questions.

Line-reference questions first

They're faster and teach you the passage.

Proof or it's wrong

Every right answer is backed by specific words. Extreme words (always, never, entirely) are usually traps.

Paired passages

Passage A questions, then B, then both.

Time check

About 10 minutes per passage set. Move on at 11.

4Science tactics

Data first

Most questions only need a figure. Read axes, units and the legend, then answer.

Triage passages

Data-heavy passages (graphs/tables) are fastest; do conflicting viewpoints last if they slow you down.

Trends language

Increases, decreases, increases then levels off, no clear relationship. Match the choice wording to the data.

Experiments

Independent = changed, dependent = measured, controls = kept the same. "Which change would test…": change only ONE variable.

Viewpoints

Summarize each scientist in one line, note what they agree on.

Don't overthink

Outside knowledge rarely helps; the answer is in the data.

5Guessing and test day

Letter of the day

Pick one letter and use it for every blind guess. It's faster than deciding each time, and there's no penalty.

Last 2 minutes

Fill every blank. Then return to any flagged question you were close on.

Breaks

Use them: snack, water, stretch. Don't review answers in your head.

Bring

Admission ticket, photo ID, permitted calculator with fresh batteries, No. 2 pencils (paper test), watch without alarms, snack.
