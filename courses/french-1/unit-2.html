Les gens et les choses

This unit is about things and people: naming them with the right little word (le, la, un, une…), describing them, and saying what you have. The big idea is gender: every French noun is masculine or feminine, and the words around it change to match.

In this unit

Articles and gender · plurals · the verb avoir and il y a · adjectives (agreement and position) · family and possessives (mon, ma, mes…).

Habit to build

Always learn a noun with its article: not "livre" but "un livre", not "table" but "une table". The article tells you the gender.

2.1Gender and articles

le, la, l', les and un, une, des.

French has two genders, masculine and feminine. For people the gender usually matches (un garçon, une fille). For things it just has to be learned: un livre (a book) is masculine, une table is feminine.

MasculineFeminineBefore a vowel or silent hPlural
the (definite)le livrela tablel'ami, l'écoleles livres
a / some (indefinite)un livreune tableun ami, une écoledes livres

Clues to gender

Often masculine

Words ending in -age (le fromage), -ment (le moment), -eau (le bureau), -isme, and most ending in a consonant.

Often feminine

Words ending in -tion / -sion (la nation), -té (la liberté), -ette, -ure (la voiture), -ence, and many ending in -e.

Watch outThese are only patterns: la plage (beach) and l'eau (water) are feminine, le musée is masculine. When you're not sure, check a dictionary for n.m. or n.f.

Using the definite article

French uses le / la / les much more than English uses the. Use it for things in general and for likes and dislikes.

J'aime le chocolat.I like chocolate. (chocolate in general)
Les chats sont mignons.Cats are cute.
Detailed explanationMore about articles

Why learn the article with the noun

Nouns that start with a vowel hide their gender with the definite article: l'ami could be either. Learn them with un / une: un ami, une école.

des is the plural of un / une

English often uses no word at all: J'ai des amis à Paris = I have friends in Paris. French almost never leaves a noun without an article.

Some nouns change meaning with gender

le livre = the book, la livre = the pound. le tour = the tour, la tour = the tower (la tour Eiffel).

2.2Making nouns plural

Usually just a silent s.

Most nouns add a silent -s, so in speech you often hear the plural only from the article: le livre vs les livres sound different because of le / les.

Il y a: there is, there are

il y a never changes for singular or plural.

Il y a un chat dans la cuisine.There's a cat in the kitchen.
Il y a vingt élèves dans la classe.There are twenty students in the class.
Qu'est-ce qu'il y a ?What's the matter? / What's there?
Detailed explanationAvoir in detail

Je becomes j'

avoir starts with a vowel, so it's always j'ai, never "je ai".

Être or avoir?

English "to be" + hungry, thirsty, hot, cold, afraid, right, wrong, sleepy, X years old → French uses avoir. For descriptions (tall, nice, tired) use être: Je suis fatigué.

Chaud and froid

For a person use avoir: J'ai chaud (I'm hot). For the weather use faire: Il fait chaud (it's hot out). For an object use être: Le café est chaud. Saying "je suis chaud" means something else, so avoid it.

2.4Describing with adjectives

Agreement and position.

Adjectives agree with the noun in gender and number. The usual pattern: add -e for feminine, -s for plural.

SingularPlural
Masculineil est grandils sont grands
Feminineelle est grandeelles sont grandes

Listen: in grande you hear the d, in grand you don't. Adding -e often makes a silent consonant come alive.

Where adjectives go

Most adjectives go after the noun: une voiture rouge, un livre intéressant. A short list goes before. Remember BAGS:

B · Beauty

beau, joli

A · Age

jeune, vieux, nouveau

G · Goodness

bon, mauvais, gentil

S · Size

grand, petit, gros

Detailed explanationTricky adjective details

bel, nouvel, vieil

Before a masculine singular noun starting with a vowel sound, beau, nouveau and vieux have a special form so the words link smoothly: un bel hôtel, un nouvel ami, un vieil homme. They still sound like the feminine forms.

Mixed groups

For a group with masculine and feminine nouns, use the masculine plural: Paul et Marie sont grands.

Plural irregulars

Adjectives ending in -s or -x don't change in the masculine plural (heureux, français); -eau becomes -eaux (beaux); -al becomes -aux (normaux).

Colors that don't change

Colors that are also nouns for things, like orange and marron (brown, from the chestnut), never change: des chaussures marron.

2.7My, your, his, her: possessives

mon, ma, mes and the rest.

A possessive agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner. sa mère can mean his mother or her mother: mère is feminine, so it's sa either way.

MasculineFemininePlural
mymon frèrema sœurmes parents
your (tu)ton frèreta sœurtes parents
his / her / itsson frèresa sœurses parents
ournotre famillenos parents
your (vous)votre famillevos parents
theirleur familleleurs parents
Before a vowelBefore a feminine word starting with a vowel, use mon / ton / son so the words link: mon amie, ton école, son histoire.

de for possession

French has no 's. Use de: le livre de Marie (Marie's book), la voiture de mon père (my father's car).

Detailed explanationPossessives in detail

Why "son" for "her"?

French looks at the noun after the possessive. son père = his father or her father. Context tells you whose. To be clear, add à lui (his) or à elle (hers): C'est son livre à elle.

Body parts

French usually uses le / la / les, not the possessive, for body parts when it's clear whose they are: J'ai les yeux bleus, Il a mal à la tête (his head hurts).

leur vs leurs

leur = their (one thing): leur maison. leurs = their (several things): leurs enfants.

Unit 2 summary

Everything from this unit on one screen.

Articles

le / la / l' / les (the) · un / une / des (a, some). Learn every noun with un or une.

Avoir

j'ai, tu as, il a, nous avons, vous avez, ils ont. avoir faim / soif / chaud / froid / peur / … ans. il y a = there is / are.

Adjectives

Agree in gender and number (+e, +s). Most go after the noun; BAGS adjectives go before.

Possessives

mon / ma / mes… agree with the thing owned. mon before a vowel. de for 's: le chat de Léa.

Common mistakes

Points students lose most often.

  1. Making the possessive agree with the owner: Marie et son frère is right (frère is masculine), not "sa frère".
  2. Forgetting agreement: une maison blanc → une maison blanche.
  3. Putting a color before the noun: une rouge voiture → une voiture rouge.
  4. Using être instead of avoir: je suis faim → j'ai faim.
  5. Saying ma amie: before a vowel use mon amie.
  6. Mixing up ils ont (they have, z sound) and ils sont (they are, s sound).
  7. Leaving out the article: J'aime chocolat → J'aime le chocolat.
