Belgium and SwitzerlandIn Belgium and Switzerland people say septante (70) and nonante (90), and in Switzerland often huitante (80). In France, stick with soixante-dix and quatre-vingt-dix.
Ça coûte quatre-vingt-quinze euros.It costs 95 euros.
Ma grand-mère a soixante-dix-huit ans.My grandmother is 78.
Detailed explanationHow to read big numbers fast

Split it

For 70–79 think "60 and something": 76 = soixante + seize. For 80–99 think "four twenties and something": 93 = quatre-vingt + treize.

Years

Years are said as one big number: 2026 = deux mille vingt-six; 1998 = mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-huit.

Prices

French uses a comma for decimals: 3,50 € = trois euros cinquante. Thousands get a space or a period: 1 500 or 1.500.

4.2Telling time

Quelle heure est-il ?

FrenchEnglish
Quelle heure est-il ?What time is it?
Il est une heure.It's one o'clock.
Il est deux heures dix.It's 2:10.
Il est trois heures et quart.It's 3:15. (quarter past)
Il est quatre heures et demie.It's 4:30. (half past)
Il est six heures moins le quart.It's 5:45. (six minus a quarter)
Il est huit heures moins dix.It's 7:50. (eight minus ten)
Il est midi. / Il est minuit.It's noon. / It's midnight.
À quelle heure… ?At what time…?

After half past, French counts down to the next hour with moins. Schedules, TV and trains use the 24-hour clock: Le train part à dix-sept heures quarante. (5:40 p.m.)

Common mistakeAlways say heure(s): Il est trois is incomplete; say Il est trois heures. And it's une heure (feminine), not un heure.

Days and months are lowercase. Dates use le + number + month, and the 1st is le premier.

Nous sommes le vendredi 14 juillet.It's Friday, July 14th. (Bastille Day)
Mon anniversaire, c'est le premier mai.My birthday is May 1st.
Le samedi, je joue au foot.On Saturdays I play soccer. (le + day = every week)

Writing dates

French writes day / month / year: 03/07/2026 is the 3rd of July, not March 7th.

le samedi vs samedi

Samedi, je vais au cinéma = this Saturday (once). Le samedi, je vais au cinéma = on Saturdays (every week).

Asking the date

Quelle est la date aujourd'hui ? or On est le combien ? Answer: On est le 12. / C'est le 12 octobre.

4.5Food and the partitive: du, de la, des

du pain, de la confiture, de l'eau.

When you talk about some of something you can't count (bread, water, cheese), French uses the partitive article. English often uses no word at all: "I want bread" → Je veux du pain.

MasculineFeminineBefore a vowelPlural
du painde la confiturede l'eaudes fraises

Which article?

le / la / les: in general, likes

J'aime le fromage. (cheese in general)

du / de la: some, a portion

Je mange du fromage. (some cheese)

Detailed explanationChoosing the right article for food

Verbs of liking take le / la / les

aimer, adorer, préférer, détester talk about things in general: Je déteste les épinards.

Verbs of eating, buying, having often take du / de la

manger, boire, prendre, acheter, vouloir talk about a portion: Je prends de la salade. But for a whole countable item, use un / une: Je mange une pomme.

The negative still uses le / la after aimer

Je n'aime pas le café (le stays: it's still "coffee in general"), but Je ne bois pas de café.

4.8Venir: to come

Coming from somewhere, and having just done something.

je viensnous venons
tu viensvous venez
il / elle vientils / elles viennent
Tu viens avec nous ?Are you coming with us?
Je viens du Canada. / Elle vient de Paris.I'm from Canada. / She's from Paris. (venir de = come from)
Je viens de manger.I just ate. (venir de + infinitive = to have just done)

revenir (to come back) and devenir (to become) follow the same pattern.

Unit 4 summary

Everything from this unit on one screen.

Numbers

70 = soixante-dix, 80 = quatre-vingts, 90 = quatre-vingt-dix, 100 = cent, 1000 = mille.

Time and dates

Il est trois heures et quart / et demie / moins le quart. le lundi = on Mondays. le premier mai. en été, au printemps.

Food

du / de la / de l' / des = some. le / la / les after aimer. de after quantities and negatives.

Town

à + le = au, à + les = aux, de + le = du, de + les = des. venir de + place / + infinitive.

Common mistakes

Points students lose most often.

  1. à le and de le never appear: always au and du.
  2. Capitalizing days and months: Lundi → lundi (unless it starts a sentence).
  3. le un mai → le premier mai.
  4. Using du after a quantity: beaucoup du sucre → beaucoup de sucre.
  5. Using du after aimer: J'aime du chocolat → J'aime le chocolat.
  6. quatre-vingts-un → quatre-vingt-un (the s drops when a number follows).
  7. Weather with être: C'est froid → Il fait froid.
