Au présent

Now you start talking about what people do. French verbs change their ending for each subject, but most of them follow one of three patterns. Learn the pattern once and you can use hundreds of verbs.

One present tense

French has one present tense for three English ones: je parle = I speak, I'm speaking, I do speak.

3.1Regular -er verbs

One pattern for hundreds of verbs.

About 90% of French verbs end in -er, and almost all of them follow this pattern.

je parle-enous parlons-ons
tu parles-esvous parlez-ez
il / elle parle-eils / elles parlent-ent (silent!)

Four of the six forms sound exactly the same: parle, parles, parle, parlent. Only the nous and vous forms sound different.

Spelling changesVerbs in -ger and -cer keep their soft sound before -ons: nous mangeons, nous commençons. A few verbs change their accent: j'achète (acheter), je préfère (préférer), je m'appelle (two l's), but nous achetons, nous préférons.
Detailed explanationWhy -ent is silent

Spelling vs sound

The -ent ending of ils / elles is never pronounced on a verb. Ils parlent sounds like il parle. You can still hear the plural with a liaison: ils‿aiment (il-zem) vs il aime.

Don't translate "do" and "am"

"Do you speak French?" is Tu parles français ? "I'm watching TV" is Je regarde la télé. No extra word for do or am.

Verbs with built-in prepositions

écouter = to listen to, regarder = to look at, chercher = to look for: J'écoute la radio. But téléphoner à needs à: Je téléphone à ma mère.

3.2Saying what you like to do

aimer + infinitive, and jouer à / de.

Put an infinitive right after aimer, adorer, préférer or détester.

J'aime nager.I like to swim / I like swimming.
Nous adorons danser.We love dancing.
Tu préfères lire ou regarder un film ?Do you prefer reading or watching a movie?

With a noun, use the definite article: J'aime le foot. Je déteste les maths.

Jouer à and jouer de

jouer à + a sport or game; jouer de + an instrument. Remember the contractions: à + le = au, de + le = du.

Je joue au foot et au basket.I play soccer and basketball.
Elle joue du piano et de la guitare.She plays the piano and the guitar.

3.3Ne … pas: making a sentence negative

ne … pas, and the de rule.

Put ne before the conjugated verb and pas after it. Ne becomes n' before a vowel.

Un, une, des become de

After a negative, the indefinite articles un, une, des (and du / de la, Unit 4) become de (d' before a vowel). le, la, les don't change.

J'ai un frère. → Je n'ai pas de frère.I have a brother. → I don't have a brother.
Il y a des élèves. → Il n'y a pas d'élèves.There are students. → There are no students.
J'aime les maths. → Je n'aime pas les maths.le / la / les stay.

In conversationPeople often drop the ne when speaking casually: Je sais pas (I dunno). Always write it, and always use it on tests.
Detailed explanationNegation in detail

Where exactly do ne and pas go?

Around the conjugated verb only. With two verbs, they wrap the first: Je n'aime pas nager. In the passé composé (Unit 5) they wrap the helper verb: Je n'ai pas mangé.

Why de?

"I don't have a brother" really means "I have zero brothers", so the article of quantity becomes de, the "zero" form. Exception: after être the article stays: Ce n'est pas un chat.

Answering a negative question

To say "yes" to a negative question, use si: — Tu n'aimes pas le chocolat ? — Si ! (Yes I do!)

The easiest pattern: question word + est-ce que + statement. Où est-ce que tu travailles ? (Where do you work?)

Quel agrees

quel (which / what) agrees with the noun: quel livre, quelle heure, quels films, quelles chansons. All four sound the same.

Common mistakeOù (where) has an accent; ou (or) doesn't. And don't confuse qu'est-ce que (what?) with est-ce que (turns a statement into a yes / no question).

Detailed explanationPatterns inside the irregulars

Same endings, odd stems

Most irregular verbs still end in -s, -s, -t in the singular (je veux, tu veux, il veut) and -ons, -ez, -ent in the plural. What changes is the stem.

Three special vous forms

Only three verbs don't end in -ez for vous: vous êtes, vous faites, vous dites (dire, to say).

Verbs like prendre

apprendre (to learn) and comprendre (to understand) work exactly like prendre: je comprends, nous comprenons, ils comprennent.

Common mistakes

Points students lose most often.

  1. Pronouncing the -ent of ils parlent. It's silent.
  2. Adding a word for do / am: Je suis regarde → Je regarde.
  3. Forgetting to change the article: Je n'ai pas un chien → Je n'ai pas de chien.
  4. Putting pas in the wrong place with two verbs: Je n'aime nager pas → Je n'aime pas nager.
  5. Forgetting -t- in inversion: Parle il ? → Parle-t-il ?
  6. nous mangons → nous mangeons; vous faisez → vous faites.
  7. jouer à for instruments: Je joue au piano → Je joue du piano.
