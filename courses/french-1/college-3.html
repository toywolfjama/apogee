Futur et conditionnel

College French: the futur simple and its irregular stems, quand + futur, the conditionnel, and all three types of si clauses.

Le futur et le conditionnel

This unit covers talking about the future precisely, being polite, and imagining things that aren't (or weren't) true. Both new tenses use the same stem, so learning one gives you most of the other.

C3.1The futur simple

je parlerai, je serai, j'irai.

Stem = the infinitive (drop the final -e of -re verbs). Endings = the present of avoir: -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont.

Je parlerai, tu finiras, il vendra, nous prendrons, vous écrirez, ils boiront.I will speak, you'll finish, he'll sell, we'll take, you'll write, they'll drink.

Futur simple vs futur proche

The futur proche (je vais partir) is for plans and things about to happen, and is more common in speech. The futur simple (je partirai) is more formal, more distant, or used for predictions and promises: Un jour, je serai médecin.

After quand, lorsque, dès que, aussitôt que

When the main verb is future, these time words are followed by the future too, unlike English: Quand je serai grand, je voyagerai. (When I grow up…) Appelle-moi dès que tu arriveras.

Detailed explanationThe futur antérieur

Will have done

Form: avoir / être in the futur simple + past participle. J'aurai fini à six heures. (I'll have finished by six.)

With quand and dès que

For an action that will be completed before another future one: Quand tu auras fini tes devoirs, tu pourras sortir. English says "when you have finished".

Si never takes the future

The time words above take the future, but si (if) never does: Si tu viens, not "si tu viendras".

C3.2The conditionnel

would, could, should.

Same stem as the future + the imparfait endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient.

Je parlerais, je serais, j'aurais, j'irais, je ferais, je pourrais, je voudrais.I would speak, be, have, go, do, be able to, want.

When to use it

Politeness

Je voudrais un café. Pourriez-vous m'aider ? Tu devrais te reposer. (You should rest.)

Wishes and hypotheses

J'aimerais vivre à Paris. À ta place, je partirais. (If I were you, I'd leave.)

Future in the past

Il a dit qu'il viendrait. (He said he would come.)

Unconfirmed news

Le président serait malade. (The president is reportedly ill.) Common in journalism.

Futur vs conditionnel for jeje parlerai (will) and je parlerais (would) sound almost alike in fast speech. In writing, check the ending: -ai = future, -ais = conditional.

C3.3Si clauses: if … then

Three patterns that cover almost every if-sentence.

TypeSi clauseResult clauseExample
1. Realprésentfutur (or présent, impératif)S'il pleut, je resterai chez moi.
2. HypotheticalimparfaitconditionnelSi j'étais toi, j'accepterais.
3. Past unrealplus-que-parfaitconditionnel passéSi tu m'avais appelé, je serais venu.

The conditionnel passé

avoir / être in the conditionnel + past participle: j'aurais aimé (I would have liked), elle serait partie (she would have left). Also used for regrets: J'aurais dû étudier. (I should have studied.) Tu aurais pu me le dire ! (You could have told me!)

The golden ruleNever use the futur or the conditionnel right after si meaning if. Si j’aurais su… is a famous mistake; it's Si j’avais su… (If I had known…).
Detailed explanationSi clause details

Si + il

si elides only before il and ils: s'il, s'ils, but si elle, si on.

Mixed time frames

You can mix types when the logic requires it: Si j'avais étudié, je serais médecin aujourd'hui (past condition, present result: plus-que-parfait + conditionnel présent).

Si meaning whether

When si means "whether" (indirect question), the future and conditional are fine: Je ne sais pas s'il viendra.

Quand + futur

Common mistakes

Points students lose most often.

  1. Si j'aurais → Si j'avais.
  2. Quand je suis grand, je serai… → Quand je serai grand…
  3. je vendreai → je vendrai (drop the -e of -re verbs).
  4. j'allerai → j'irai; je fairai → je ferai.
  5. Confusing -ai (future) and -ais (conditional) in writing.
  6. J'aurais dû étudier means "I should have studied", not "I would have to".
