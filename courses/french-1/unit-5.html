Hier et demain

So far everything has been in the present. In this unit you learn to talk about tomorrow (the futur proche) and yesterday (the passé composé). Both tenses are built from two words: a helper verb you already know plus a second verb.

In this unit

The futur proche · the passé composé with avoir · with être · negatives and questions in the past · reflexive verbs and your daily routine · words for telling a story in order.

Habit to build

Every evening, say three sentences out loud about what you did today and one about what you're going to do tomorrow.

5.1The futur proche: going to do something

aller + infinitive.

Conjugate aller in the present and add an infinitive. That's it.

Je vais étudier ce soir.I'm going to study tonight.
Nous allons voyager en France cet été.We're going to travel to France this summer.
Ils vont manger au restaurant.They're going to eat at the restaurant.

Negative

ne … pas wraps aller: Je ne vais pas sortir. (I'm not going to go out.)

Detailed explanationMore on the futur proche

When to use it

For plans and things about to happen. French also has a one-word future (je parlerai), which you'll learn in French 2; the futur proche is more common in conversation.

With reflexive verbs

The reflexive pronoun goes with the infinitive and matches the subject: Je vais me lever à 7 h. Tu vas te coucher ?

Don't confuse with aller + place

Je vais au parc = I'm going to the park (movement). Je vais jouer au parc = I'm going to play in the park (plan).

5.2The passé composé with avoir

j'ai mangé, tu as fini, il a vendu.

The passé composé tells what happened: I ate, I have eaten, I did eat. Build it from avoir in the present + a past participle.

-er-éparler → parlé: j'ai parlé
-ir-ifinir → fini: tu as fini

Detailed explanationUsing the passé composé

One French tense, three English ones

J'ai mangé can mean I ate, I have eaten, or I did eat. Don't add words: "I have eaten" is still just j'ai mangé.

Placement of adverbs

Short adverbs go between the helper verb and the participle: J'ai bien dormi (I slept well), Il a déjà mangé (he already ate), Tu as beaucoup travaillé.

Questions

Intonation or est-ce que work as usual: Tu as fini ?, Est-ce que tu as fini ? For inversion, swap the helper verb and subject: As-tu fini ?, A-t-il mangé ?

Agreement

With être, the participle agrees with the subject like an adjective: add -e for feminine, -s for plural.

Ma sœur est née en 2010.My sister was born in 2010.
Nous sommes rentrés tard.We got home late.
Detailed explanationÊtre verbs in detail

The house of être

Another way to remember them: picture a house. You're born (naître), arrive, enter, go up the stairs (monter), stay, go down (descendre), fall, go out, leave, return, and die (mourir). The verbs describe moving in, out, up and down.

vous and on

vous can be one person or several: Vous êtes allé (one man, formal), Vous êtes allées (several women). With on meaning "we", many writers agree in the plural: On est partis.

Not every movement verb

marcher (walk), courir (run), voyager and nager use avoir: J'ai marché. Only the list verbs take être.

5.4Reflexive verbs and your daily routine

se lever, s'habiller, se coucher.

Reflexive verbs have an extra pronoun that refers back to the subject: je me lève (I get up, literally "I raise myself").

je me lèvenous nous levons
tu te lèvesvous vous levez
il / elle se lèveils / elles se lèvent

me, te, se become m', t', s' before a vowel: je m'habille, il s'appelle.

In the past

All reflexive verbs use être in the passé composé, and the participle usually agrees: Elle s'est levée à sept heures. The pronoun stays right before être.

Negative

ne goes before the pronoun: Je ne me lève pas tôt. Il ne s'est pas douché.

Detailed explanationReflexive verbs in detail

Same verb, two meanings

Je lave la voiture (I wash the car) vs Je me lave (I wash myself). Many verbs can be used both ways.

Body parts

With a reflexive verb, use le / la / les for body parts: Je me brosse les dents, not "mes dents".

Infinitives

After another verb, the pronoun still matches the subject: J'aime me promener, Nous devons nous lever tôt.

Unit 5 summary

Everything from this unit on one screen.

Futur proche

aller + infinitive: je vais manger. Negative: je ne vais pas manger.

Passé composé (avoir)

avoir + participle: -er → é, -ir → i, -re → u. Irregulars: eu, été, fait, pris, mis, vu, bu, lu, écrit.

Passé composé (être)

DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP + all reflexive verbs. The participle agrees: elle est allée, ils sont partis.

Negatives and order

ne … pas wraps the helper verb: je n'ai pas fini, elle n'est pas venue. Time words: hier, d'abord, ensuite, enfin.

Common mistakes

Points students lose most often.

  1. Using avoir with an être verb: J'ai allé → Je suis allé(e).
  2. Forgetting agreement with être: Elle est parti → Elle est partie.
  3. Making avoir verbs agree with the subject: Elle a mangée → Elle a mangé.
  4. Putting pas after the participle: Je n'ai mangé pas → Je n'ai pas mangé.
  5. Regular-izing irregulars: j'ai prendu → j'ai pris; j'ai faisé → j'ai fait.
  6. Futur proche with a conjugated second verb: Je vais mange → Je vais manger.
  7. Reflexive pronoun not matching: Nous se levons → Nous nous levons.
